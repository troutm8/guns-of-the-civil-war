<dl class="glossary">
<dt>Bands</dt>
<dd>Metal rings that hold the barrel to the stock. A "three-band" rifle-musket has a long 39- or 40-inch barrel; a "two-band" rifle is shorter.</dd>

<dt>Bore</dt>
<dd>The inside of the barrel. Its diameter, in hundredths of an inch, is the arm's caliber.</dd>

<dt>Breechloader</dt>
<dd>An arm loaded from the rear of the barrel rather than the muzzle.</dd>

<dt>Buck and ball</dt>
<dd>A smoothbore load of one round ball and three buckshot, deadly at short range.</dd>

<dt>Caliber</dt>
<dd>The diameter of the bore, in inches. A .58-caliber arm has a bore 0.58 in. across.</dd>

<dt>Cap pouch</dt>
<dd>The small leather pouch on the waist belt that held percussion caps.</dd>

<dt>Carbine</dt>
<dd>A short, light shoulder arm, usually issued to cavalry.</dd>

<dt>Cartridge box</dt>
<dd>The leather box, carried on a sling or the waist belt, holding forty rounds in tin liners.</dd>

<dt>Cone (nipple)</dt>
<dd>The hollow, threaded tube at the breech on which the percussion cap is placed.</dd>

<dt>Farb</dt>
<dd>Reenactor's slang for anything inauthentic in kit or conduct.</dd>

<dt>Furniture</dt>
<dd>The metal fittings of an arm's stock: butt plate, trigger guard, bands, nose cap.</dd>

<dt>Galvanize</dt>
<dd>To portray the other side for an event. The term originally described Confederate prisoners who enlisted in Federal service.</dd>

<dt>Half-cock</dt>
<dd>The safety notch of the hammer, in which the trigger will not release it. Arms are capped at half-cock.</dd>

<dt>Impression</dt>
<dd>The particular soldier a reenactor portrays: side, branch, unit, theater and year.</dd>

<dt>Lock</dt>
<dd>The firing mechanism: lock plate, hammer, mainspring, tumbler and sear.</dd>

<dt>Minié ball</dt>
<dd>The hollow-based conical bullet of the rifle-musket, named after Captain Claude-Étienne Minié.</dd>

<dt>Musket</dt>
<dd>Strictly, a smoothbore long arm. In wartime usage, often any infantry shoulder arm.</dd>

<dt>Percussion cap</dt>
<dd>A small copper cup containing fulminate, which fires the charge when struck by the hammer.</dd>

<dt>Rammer (ramrod)</dt>
<dd>The steel rod carried beneath the barrel, used to seat the bullet on the powder.</dd>

<dt>Repeater</dt>
<dd>An arm that could fire several shots from a magazine without reloading, such as the Spencer or the Henry.</dd>

<dt>Rifle-musket</dt>
<dd>A long, rifled muzzle-loader of musket length, able to take a socket bayonet. The Springfield and the Enfield are the chief examples.</dd>

<dt>Rimfire</dt>
<dd>A metallic cartridge with its priming compound in the rim of the case.</dd>

<dt>Rifling</dt>
<dd>Spiral grooves cut inside the bore to spin the bullet, making it fly true.</dd>

<dt>Smoothbore</dt>
<dd>A barrel without rifling.</dd>

<dt>Spark shield</dt>
<dd>A modern hammer shroud, fitted to some reenactors' arms to protect neighbors in the ranks from cap fragments.</dd>

<dt>Tompion</dt>
<dd>A plug placed in the muzzle to keep out rain and dirt.</dd>

<dt>Worm</dt>
<dd>A spiral wire tool screwed onto the rammer to draw out a cartridge or cleaning patch.</dd>
</dl>

Samuel Griswold was a Connecticut-born cotton-gin maker who had built a thriving industrial village near Macon, Georgia. In 1862, with Arvin Gunnison, he turned his works to making revolvers for the Confederacy.

## Development

The Griswold & Gunnison copied the Colt Navy's mechanism, but with a **brass frame** to save iron, which the South could not spare. The barrel is round rather than octagonal, with a lug like that of the Colt Dragoon. Many of the workmen were enslaved, a fact the reenactor portraying this arm's history should understand.

## In the Field

Griswold revolvers were issued chiefly to Confederate cavalry in Georgia and the Western Theater. Production ended in November 1864, when Federal cavalry burned Griswoldville during Sherman's march.

## For the Reenactor

A brass-framed "Reb Navy" reproduction is a good choice for **Confederate cavalry**, and a truer one than a Colt copy for most enlisted men. Keep the frame's shine subdued. Brass revolvers in the field were not kept polished.

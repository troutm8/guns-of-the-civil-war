The Sharps carbine was the backbone of the Federal cavalry's firepower until the Spencer replaced it in 1864. It shared the rifle's falling-block action and .52 caliber in a much shorter, lighter arm that a trooper could carry on a sling at his side and load in the saddle.

## In the Field

Federal cavalrymen at Brandy Station and at Gettysburg on July 1, 1863, most famously John Buford's troopers, fought largely with Sharps carbines, together with Burnsides, Merrills and others. The Sharps was valued for accuracy and ruggedness and kept its reputation to the end of the war.

## Confederate Copies

The Confederacy prized captured Sharps. In Richmond, **S.C. Robinson Arms Manufactory** made close copies from 1862, later taken over by the Confederate government. These Richmond Sharps were plainer and generally inferior, but they were among the few breech-loading arms the South produced in any number.

## For the Reenactor

The Sharps carbine is correct for **Federal cavalry** from 1862 on and, by capture, for Confederate cavalry. Carry it on a **carbine sling** with a swivel snap, and mind the muzzle when mounting and dismounting. The sling was designed to keep the carbine with the trooper, not to leave it swinging into the man beside him.

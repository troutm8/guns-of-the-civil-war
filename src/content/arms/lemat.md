Jean Alexandre LeMat, a New Orleans physician, patented his revolver in 1856. The Confederacy contracted for it in 1861. Because the South had no factory able to make it, the revolvers were built in France and Belgium and brought through the blockade.

## Development

The LeMat's cylinder held **nine** .42-caliber charges and revolved around a central barrel that served as the cylinder pin. That lower barrel was a smoothbore that could fire a load of buckshot. A movable nose on the hammer let the shooter choose which barrel to fire.

## In the Field

Only about 2,900 were made, and far fewer reached the Confederacy. The LeMat is most often associated with senior officers. P.G.T. Beauregard and J.E.B. Stuart are both said to have owned one, and its fame greatly exceeds its numbers.

## For the Reenactor

A LeMat is a **general officer's or staff officer's** arm. In the hands of a private cavalryman it is almost certainly out of place, and most units will regard it as "farby" (inauthentic) unless your impression calls for it.

Ambrose Burnside resigned from the army in 1853 to manufacture a carbine of his own design. The venture failed, and he sold his patent rights to satisfy his creditors. The carbine went into large-scale production only after he had rejoined the army as a general, and he never profited by it.

## Development

The Burnside was among the first American arms to use a **metallic cartridge**. The tapered brass case was loaded from the front of the tilting breech block. It was not self-primed: a standard musket cap fired the charge through a small hole in the base of the case. The brass case sealed the breech against gas far better than the Sharps's linen cartridge, though the extraction of spent cases could be slow. Five models were made, the Fifth being the most numerous.

## In the Field

The Burnside was the third most widely issued Union carbine, after the Spencer and the Sharps. It saw much service in the Western Theater and was carried by many Rhode Island and Ohio cavalry regiments.

## For the Reenactor

A Burnside suits **Federal cavalry** of any year and makes a pleasant change from the ubiquitous Sharps. Ask your unit how it handles blanks for a cartridge-fed carbine. Many units have an approved method, and it must be followed exactly.

The Model 1863 was not a new design so much as the Model 1861 refined by two years of wartime manufacture. The Armory's engineers borrowed several features from Colt's "Special Model" contract musket and cut out the steps that slowed production.

## The Two Types

The **Type I** (1863) used a case-hardened lock plate and hammer that show a mottled color. It had solid, clamping barrel bands held by screws in place of band springs, and a straight-sided hammer. The **Type II** (1864) went back to band springs and flat bands, and replaced the two-leaf rear sight with a single leaf graduated to 500 yards. Collectors and older books sometimes call the Type II the "Model 1864", but the Ordnance Department never used that name.

## In the Field

The Model 1863 reached the armies from the summer of 1863 onward. It armed many of the regiments raised or re-equipped in 1864, and it was in the hands of Federal infantry at the Wilderness, Petersburg, Atlanta and Nashville. In the field it performed exactly like its predecessor, and most soldiers would not have known the difference.

## For the Reenactor

A Type I or Type II is correct only for a **late-war** Federal impression. If your unit portrays the same regiment across the whole war, an 1861 Springfield is the more flexible purchase. Reproductions of the Type II are sometimes offered. Make sure you are not buying the two-band Remington "Zouave" rifle of 1863, a .58 arm of a different pattern that saw little service.

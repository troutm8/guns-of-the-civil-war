Christian Sharps patented his breech-loading system in 1848. By 1859 the design had matured into the "New Model", a sturdy falling-block arm operated by the trigger guard. Pulling the guard down drops the breech block. The soldier inserts a combustible cartridge and closes the lever, and the sharp upper edge of the block shears off the rear of the cartridge to expose the powder. A cap on the cone completes the load.

## Development

The Sharps could be loaded lying down or kneeling, far more easily than any muzzle-loader, and fired eight or ten times a minute in practiced hands. Its gas seal at the breech was never perfect. A spit of flame at the joint was normal, and the Sharps was improved several times to address it. The 1863 pattern dropped the patch box of the earlier model.

## Berdan's Sharpshooters

Hiram Berdan raised the **1st and 2nd U.S. Sharpshooters** in 1861. The men were selected by a marksmanship test and dressed in green. Berdan at first was offered Springfields, then Colt revolving rifles. After much agitation the regiments received about 2,000 Sharps rifles with double-set triggers in the spring of 1862. They carried them with distinction from the Peninsula to Petersburg.

## For the Reenactor

A Sharps rifle is required for a **U.S. Sharpshooter** impression and suits skirmisher companies of other regiments. It is out of place in an ordinary line infantry company, where it will draw questions. Blanks for the Sharps must be made to suit its breech. Follow your unit's pattern carefully, and never close the breech on anything but an approved blank.

<div class="caution"><strong>Caution.</strong> Keep the breech face and the gas seal clean. A fouled Sharps spits fire at the breech, and the man on your left will feel it.</div>

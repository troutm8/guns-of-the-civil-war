When Virginia troops seized Harpers Ferry in April 1861, they carried off the machinery for making the Model 1855 rifle-musket. The machinery was shipped to Richmond, where the Virginia Manufactory of Arms (later the C.S. Armory) was fitted out to use it.

## Development

The Richmond arm copies the Model 1855, including the high **"hump"** of the lock plate that housed the Maynard tape primer. The Confederates did not make the primer mechanism itself, however. The Richmond lock is an ordinary percussion lock that keeps only the 1855's distinctive profile. Early production used Harpers Ferry parts. Later arms show brass furniture, simpler finishing and wartime economies.

## In the Field

The Richmond rifle-musket was the most important Confederate-made shoulder arm. It was issued chiefly to the Army of Northern Virginia, alongside imported Enfields and captured Springfields.

## For the Reenactor

A Richmond is a strong choice for a **Confederate infantry impression in the East**. It sets a man apart from the Enfield-carrying majority without being out of place. The hump lock is the telltale feature. Make sure your reproduction has it and carries **C.S. Richmond, VA** markings with a period date.

The Model 1841 was the first U.S. rifle made in the percussion system. Its brass furniture, walnut stock and short, heavy barrel made it one of the handsomest arms of its day. It won fame in the Mexican War when the 1st Mississippi Rifles, led by Colonel Jefferson Davis, carried it at Monterrey and Buena Vista, and "Mississippi Rifle" became its lasting nickname.

## Development

The rifle was designed for the patched round ball. After the adoption of the Minié system in 1855, many were rebored to .58 caliber, fitted with new long-range sights, and modified with a lug so they could take a saber bayonet. The alterations are various and a study in themselves.

## In the Field

In 1861 the Mississippi rifle was still in many state arsenals, and it armed a good number of regiments on both sides. It was particularly common in the Confederacy. Several Southern makers produced close copies, including the Fayetteville Arsenal and makers in Georgia and Texas.

## For the Reenactor

The Mississippi rifle is a good fit for **early-war Confederate** and state-troop impressions, and for the Trans-Mississippi. It has no socket bayonet, so check whether your unit will expect bayonet drill. A two-band rifle with a saber bayonet was a mark of rifle companies and skirmishers, and it may set you apart in the ranks.

Benjamin Tyler Henry, plant superintendent for Oliver Winchester's New Haven Arms Company, patented his rifle in 1860. It fired a .44 rimfire cartridge from a tube magazine under the barrel. A lever that doubled as the trigger guard extracted the empty case, cocked the hammer and chambered a fresh round in one motion.

## Development

The Henry's sixteen-shot capacity was unmatched. But it was costly, and its magazine was loaded from the front of an open slot that could admit dirt and be damaged. The Ordnance Department bought only about 1,700 for issue, most to the **1st District of Columbia Cavalry**. Many more were bought privately by soldiers, especially in Western regiments, who paid a good part of a year's wages for one.

## In the Field

Henrys appeared on many fields, especially in the Atlanta campaign and the Western armies in 1864. Some Confederates are said to have called it "that damned Yankee rifle that they load on Sunday and shoot all week", though the saying is attributed to several arms. Captured Henrys were of limited Confederate use for want of ammunition.

## For the Reenactor

A Henry is a **specialist's arm**. It is correct for a soldier who could have afforded one, or for the 1st D.C. Cavalry. It will stand out in most ranks. Reproductions are chambered for modern cartridges, and most units forbid firing them in battle scenarios. Ask before you bring one.

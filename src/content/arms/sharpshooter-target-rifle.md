Before the war, American target shooters had developed a type of **heavy muzzle-loading match rifle** of extraordinary accuracy. They were enormously heavy, fitted with a false muzzle to start the bullet true, and topped with a telescopic sight that ran the length of the barrel. Some sharpshooter companies took them to war.

## In the Field

Heavy target rifles could not be carried on the march by a man who also carried his pack, so they usually traveled by wagon. They were brought out for **sieges and static fronts**, where a marksman firing from a rest could pick off enemy gunners and sharpshooters at great distance. They were used in the Peninsula campaign of 1862, at Vicksburg and at Petersburg.

## For the Reenactor

A heavy target rifle is a **specialist display piece**, suited to a living-history camp or a sharpshooter demonstration rather than the battle line. It makes a compelling exhibit for the public, especially with a period rest, loading tools and a good account of how it was used.

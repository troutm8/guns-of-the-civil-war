Remington's revolver was designed by Fordyce Beals, and the familiar "1858" name comes from a patent date stamped on the barrel. The earlier Beals and 1861 models led to the **New Model Army** of 1863, the pattern bought in quantity for the Federal cavalry.

## Development

The Remington's great advantage over the Colt was its **solid frame**. A top strap over the cylinder made the revolver stronger and gave it a proper rear sight groove. The cylinder could be removed quickly by dropping the loading lever and drawing the base pin. A trooper could, in principle, carry a loaded spare cylinder and swap it in the field, though whether many did so in the war is doubtful.

## In the Field

After the Colt factory fire in 1864, the Remington became the chief revolver bought by the government. It was issued widely to Federal cavalry in the war's final two years.

## For the Reenactor

The Remington suits **Federal cavalry and officers** from 1863 on. Take care with early-war impressions. The "1858" name on the reproduction box does not make it correct for 1861.

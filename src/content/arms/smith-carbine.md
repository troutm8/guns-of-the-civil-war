Gilbert Smith of Buttermilk Falls, New York, patented his carbine in 1855 and 1856. Its barrel hinged down at the breech, like a shotgun's, when a latch in front of the trigger guard was pressed. The trooper then inserted a cartridge directly into the chamber.

## Development

The Smith's cartridge was its most distinctive feature. Early cartridges had a case of **India rubber**, which expanded to seal the breech on firing and then contracted so that it could be pulled out by hand. Foil and paper cartridges were also made. Like the Burnside, the Smith was fired by an ordinary musket cap, whose flash passed through a hole in the base of the cartridge.

## In the Field

About 30,000 Smiths were delivered. They armed a number of Federal cavalry regiments, especially in the East. Troopers generally liked the carbine, though the rubber cartridges could stick in a hot, fouled chamber and had to be pried out.

## For the Reenactor

The Smith is another good choice for a **Federal cavalry** impression, and reproductions are available. As with every cartridge-fed carbine, follow your unit's rules for blanks to the letter.

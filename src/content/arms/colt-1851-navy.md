The Colt Navy, so called for the naval battle scene rolled onto its cylinder, was the revolver of choice for a generation of Americans before the war. Its lighter .36 caliber made it handier than the .44 Army, and many officers of both armies bought one privately.

## In the Field

The Navy was bought in quantity by both governments in 1861. It was especially favored in the Confederate cavalry, where many men carried two or more revolvers. It was also the model most copied by Southern makers. Griswold & Gunnison, Leech & Rigdon, Spiller & Burr and others made close copies, often with brass frames to save scarce iron.

## For the Reenactor

The 1851 Navy is correct for **officers** of either side and for **Confederate cavalry** throughout the war. It is one of the easiest reproductions to find. For a Confederate impression, consider a reproduction of one of the Southern copies.

The Model 1854 rifle designed by Joseph Lorenz was the standard arm of the Austrian Empire in the 1850s. When the Austrian army began to rearm after the war of 1859, large stocks were declared surplus, and American agents of both governments bought them eagerly.

## Development

The Lorenz used an unusual rifling system, with shallow grooves and a projectile designed to be crushed into them by the ramrod. In American service most were issued with ordinary Minié ammunition. Many were rebored to .58 caliber before issue so they could take the standard U.S. cartridge; others were issued in the original .54 bore, which complicated supply.

Quality varied enormously. The best Austrian-made Lorenz rifles were sturdy and accurate. The worst, often the second-class arms dumped on the market, were poorly made, with soft metal, rough bores and sights set so badly that one officer reported they would not hit the side of a barn. Regiments issued Austrians in 1862 often counted the day they were exchanged for Springfields or Enfields as a blessing.

## In the Field

Lorenz rifles were especially common in the Western armies. They were carried at Shiloh, Perryville, Stones River, Vicksburg and Chickamauga. The Confederate Army of Tennessee used them in quantity through 1863.

## For the Reenactor

A Lorenz is a distinctive choice that fits Western-theater impressions for either side in 1862 and 1863. Reproductions are scarce. Some reenactors make do with period-looking substitutes, but many units will ask you to carry a Springfield or Enfield instead. **Ask before you buy.**
